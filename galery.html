How to use this page

How to use this page

Browsing? Scan the discipline headings and stop at the one closest to what you teach — the seven sections are independent, and nothing here builds on anything before it.

Reading through? Work top to bottom in about ten minutes; the point of the full pass is the range, not any single image.

Short on time? Open two graphics from a discipline that is not yours. Seeing the same technique applied outside your field is what makes the capability transferable.

Select any graphic to open it larger, or use Full Size to load the original file in a new tab for projection or download.

Before you use one of these in class

Evidence standard for these graphics

Every image below was generated by an AI tool from a discipline-specific prompt. None has been through subject-matter review. Treat each one as a draft visual and check four things before it reaches students:

  1. Labels. Read every word in the image against an authoritative source in your field. Generated diagrams place plausible labels confidently, including in the wrong place.
  2. Completeness. Confirm nothing essential to your learning objective has been left out, and nothing decorative has been invented.
  3. Legibility. Project it. Type that reads on a laptop often fails at the back of a room.
  4. Description. Supply your own alt text or a spoken description; a picture alone is not accessible to every student.

A graphic that fails any of the four is still useful — as a starting point you correct, which is often faster than starting from a blank page.
