How to use this page

How to use this page

Browsing? Open the one discipline card that matches what you teach and close the page. The six discipline panels are independent; none depends on the others, and reading all thirty-six entries is not the point.

Reading through? Allow about fifteen minutes. Read for the pattern — the same handful of jobs (explain, generate, assess, search, simulate) recurs in every discipline under different product names.

New to AI tools entirely? Read one section only: Implementation Roadmap & Best Practices at the end. It is the prerequisite for the rest, and the discipline lists will make more sense after it.

Selection heuristic

Choosing from thirty-six: a five-question filter

Thirty-six options is not a menu, it is a decision problem. Run any tool you are tempted by through these five questions, in order, and stop at the first "no".

  1. Does it solve a problem you already have this term? If you cannot name the class, the assignment, and the hour it would save, the answer is no — regardless of how good the tool is.
  2. Is there a free tier you can test first? The price column in every panel below is there for this reason. Prove the fit before anything is purchased.
  3. What leaves your institution? Anything touching student work or records is a FERPA question before it is a pedagogy question. Institutional and enterprise tiers exist because of this; the free ones often do not.
  4. Does it fit the software you already run? A tool inside your LMS or office suite gets used. A tool in a twelfth browser tab does not.
  5. Can you verify its output in the time you have? If checking the result takes longer than doing the work yourself, the tool has cost you time, not saved it.

Two tools that clear all five beat ten that clear three.

What this page claims — and what it does not

Evidence standard

Each entry is a short capability summary compiled for this workshop from vendor descriptions. These are not evaluations, trials, or endorsements, and no tool here has been tested against another. Prices and feature lists are the most perishable thing on the page — confirm both, plus the privacy terms, on the vendor's own site before adopting anything. Inclusion here means a tool exists and is used in that discipline. It does not mean it is good.

Implementation guide

Getting Started with AI in Your Courses

Ready to integrate AI tools? Start small, focus on solving specific teaching challenges, and gradually expand your AI toolkit based on what works best for your students and teaching style.

Implementation Roadmap

🎯 Week 1–2

Explore

Try 2–3 free AI tools relevant to your discipline. Start with ChatGPT, Google's NotebookLM, and one subject-specific tool. Spend 30 minutes exploring each.

📝 Week 3–4

Experiment

Use AI tools for lesson planning, quiz generation, or content creation. Document what works and what doesn't. Share experiences with colleagues.

👥 Week 5–6

Introduce to Students

Demonstrate AI tools to students. Establish guidelines for appropriate use. Create assignments that leverage AI while maintaining academic integrity.

🔄 Ongoing

Iterate & Improve

Regularly assess effectiveness. Stay updated on new tools. Join faculty AI communities. Continuously refine your AI integration strategy.

Best Practices for Faculty

Do

  • Start with free tools before investing in premium ones
  • Be transparent with students about AI use
  • Use AI to enhance, not replace, human interaction
  • Verify AI-generated content for accuracy
  • Establish clear AI usage policies
  • Focus on tools that solve real problems

Don't

  • Don't rely on AI for all content creation
  • Don't ignore privacy and data concerns
  • Don't use AI without understanding how it works
  • Don't assume all AI outputs are accurate
  • Don't implement tools without training
  • Don't overwhelm students with too many tools
